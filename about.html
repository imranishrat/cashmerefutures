About

Connecting the people cashmere depends on.

Cashmere Futures is an emerging platform bringing together perspectives from cashmere communities, research, industry and policy — working toward practical, evidence-based pathways for a more sustainable, inclusive and transformative cashmere sector.

Most people in the cashmere sector see one part of the picture. We're building a place to see more of it.

[Draft — please edit] Herders know the land and the animals. Researchers study fibre quality, climate pressure, and market structure. Buyers and brands set demand. Policymakers shape the rules everyone operates under. Each group holds knowledge the others need — but rarely in one place, and rarely in a form the others can use.

Cashmere Futures exists to narrow that gap: a shared, evolving picture of the sector that's honest about what's still uncertain, and useful to whoever's trying to make it better.

How we work

01 — 05
01

Community-first

[Draft] Herding communities are treated as primary stakeholders, not a data source — their priorities shape what we look into.

02

Evidence over assumption

[Draft] We favour verifiable data and named sources over convenient narratives, and we say clearly when something is still uncertain.

03

Transformative, not just protective

[Draft] We're not only trying to preserve the status quo more sustainably — we look at what structural changes, in market access, technology or governance, could genuinely change outcomes.

04

Open to collaboration

[Draft] This platform is built to connect people already doing the work — not to replace or compete with existing efforts.

05

Transparent about limits

[Draft] We say plainly what's still early, uncertain, or not yet built — rather than presenting a more finished picture than what actually exists.

We're early, and we're building in the open.

[Draft — please edit or remove] Cashmere Futures is a young initiative. Some sections of this site — like the Research library — are still being populated. We'd rather launch honestly and grow in view than present a finished picture before the work is done.
