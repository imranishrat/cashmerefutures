From fibre to garment

How cashmere moves through the supply chain

Cashmere is transformed through a sequence of biological, processing, textile and commercial activities. The pathway is geographically dispersed and can vary by fibre quality, processing capability, ownership and market.

Supply chain stages

From herding to retailing

The sequence follows Textile Exchange's representation of the cashmere supply chain, from herding and raw-fibre processing through spinning, dyeing, weaving, garment making and retailing.

Value creation

Value is added unevenly

Economic value does not accumulate uniformly across the chain. Fibre characteristics, processing technology, market access, design, branding, certification and trading relationships can influence who captures value.

Transformation

Traceability matters

Each transition can create opportunities for better documentation, transparency and responsible production—from origin and primary processing to manufacturing and final markets.

Analytical note: This is a conceptual representation, not a single universal route. Textile Exchange notes that dyeing may occur at the yarn or fabric stage, while actual cashmere supply chains can include additional trading, storage, transport, testing, certification and finishing activities.
