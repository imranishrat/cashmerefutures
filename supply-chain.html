From fibre to garment

How cashmere moves through the supply chain

Cashmere is transformed through a sequence of biological, processing, textile and commercial activities. The pathway is geographically dispersed and can vary by fibre quality, processing capability, ownership and market.

Geographic origins

Where cashmere fibre is produced

Cashmere production is concentrated across the cold, dry regions of Central and East Asia, with smaller volumes produced in other countries.

Value creation

Value is added unevenly across the chain. Fibre quality, processing capability, craftsmanship, technology, branding and market positioning all influence where economic and symbolic value accumulates.

Transformation

Traceability matters because material transformations can occur across multiple jurisdictions and organisations. Linking origin, processing and product information can strengthen transparency and support more responsible supply-chain decisions.

Analytical note. The supply chain is not a single linear pathway. Production systems, processing locations, ownership structures and routes to market vary by country and product. The framework above provides a simplified analytical representation for examining transformation, traceability and sustainability.
